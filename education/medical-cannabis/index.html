Medical Cannabis in New Jersey: How the Program Works

By Greenstop Wellness4 min readReviewed November 2026

Here is how New Jersey's medical cannabis program works, step by step. Whether it is right for you is a conversation for you and your healthcare provider.

Educational content, not medical or legal advice. Rules change: check the official sources linked on this page.

What the program is

New Jersey's Medicinal Cannabis Program, previously called the Medicinal Marijuana Program, helps registered patients under the care of licensed healthcare practitioners access medicinal cannabis from regulated and monitored facilities. Medicinal cannabis is not covered by any health plan in New Jersey, and the program does not share a patient's enrollment information without the patient's written consent.

From official New Jersey sources

  • New Jersey's Medicinal Cannabis Program (previously the Medicinal Marijuana Program) helps registered patients under the care of licensed healthcare practitioners access cannabis-based medicine from regulated and monitored facilities. Medicinal Cannabis Program (opens the official source in a new tab)
  • Medicinal cannabis treatment is not covered by any health plan in New Jersey. Patient FAQs (opens the official source in a new tab)
  • The program does not share a patient's enrollment information without the patient's written consent. Patient FAQs (opens the official source in a new tab)

Who can register

To register as a patient, you must:

  • be a New Jersey resident,
  • be diagnosed with a qualifying medical condition by a New Jersey healthcare practitioner registered with the program, and
  • have a bona fide relationship with a healthcare provider registered with the program.

From official New Jersey sources

  • To register as a patient you must have a bona fide relationship with a healthcare provider registered with the program, be a New Jersey resident, and be diagnosed with a qualifying medical condition by a registered New Jersey healthcare practitioner. Medicinal Cannabis Program (opens the official source in a new tab)

A bona fide relationship means the practitioner has treated you for at least one year, has seen you for the condition on at least four visits, or has taken over care of the condition after a comprehensive medical history and physical exam, including a review of your records from other treating physicians. The conditions are listed in our guide to qualifying conditions.

From official New Jersey sources

  • A bona fide relationship means the practitioner has treated you for at least one year, or has seen you for the condition on at least four visits, or has taken over care of the condition after a comprehensive medical history and physical examination, including a review of your records from other treating physicians. Medicinal Cannabis Program (opens the official source in a new tab)

Adults and minors with a qualifying medical condition may participate. For a minor whose authorizing provider is not a pediatrician, a parent or guardian also needs a Minor Attestation. The official pages differ on who issues it, so check the current requirement.

From official New Jersey sources

  • Adults and minors with a qualifying medical condition may participate in the Medicinal Cannabis Program. General Information: Legal Cannabis in New Jersey (opens the official source in a new tab)
  • If a minor's authorizing provider is not a pediatrician, the parent or guardian also needs a Minor Attestation from a pediatrician. Medicinal Cannabis Program (opens the official source in a new tab)
  • The registration page says the parent or guardian of a minor patient must request a Minor Attestation from the authorizing provider if that provider is not a pediatrician. New Patient Registration (opens the official source in a new tab)

How registration works

Registration is online only, and the program's patient services representatives can walk you through setting up or accessing your account.

From official New Jersey sources

  • Registration is online only, and patient services representatives can walk you through setting up or accessing your account. Patient FAQs (opens the official source in a new tab)
  • Find a provider. Check whether your current healthcare provider participates in the program, or find a participating practitioner.
  • Get your statement. If you and your provider decide that adding cannabis to your course of treatment makes sense for you, the provider gives you an Authorizing Healthcare Practitioner Statement with a reference ID number and a registry ID number.
  • Open a portal account. You need those numbers to set up a patient portal account with a username and password.
  • Complete registration. Once the account is active, choose an Alternative Treatment Center (a dispensary) to buy from, which you can change at any time, upload proof of New Jersey residency, choose your ID card option and upload a recent photo.

From official New Jersey sources

  • Step one is to find out whether your current healthcare provider participates in the program, or to find a participating practitioner. Medicinal Cannabis Program (opens the official source in a new tab)
  • If you and your provider think cannabis will help, the provider gives you an Authorizing Healthcare Practitioner Statement with a reference ID number and a registry ID number, which you need to open your patient portal account and register. New Patient Registration (opens the official source in a new tab)
  • You then create a username and password for an account in the program's patient portal. Medicinal Cannabis Program (opens the official source in a new tab)
  • Once the account is active, you complete registration by choosing an Alternative Treatment Center (dispensary) to buy from, which you can change at any time, uploading proof of New Jersey residency, choosing your ID card option, and uploading a recent photo. Medicinal Cannabis Program (opens the official source in a new tab)

Once you have created your portal account and started registration, you may buy at a medical dispensary with your government-issued ID while your registration is under review. Renewing patients are not eligible for this.

From official New Jersey sources

  • Once you have created your portal account and started registration, you may buy at a medical dispensary with your government-issued ID while your registration is under review; renewing patients are not eligible for this. Patient FAQs (opens the official source in a new tab)

ID cards and caregivers

Digital ID cards are free for new and renewing patients and caregivers, and a physical card costs $10. Both are valid for two years. Renewal reminders are sent 60 days before your ID expires, and you may renew any time after that.

From official New Jersey sources

  • Digital ID cards are free for new and renewing patients and caregivers; a physical ID card costs $10. Medicinal Cannabis Program (opens the official source in a new tab)
  • Both digital and physical ID cards are valid for two years. New Patient Registration (opens the official source in a new tab)
  • Renewal reminders are sent 60 days before your ID expires, and you may renew at any time after that. Medicinal Cannabis Program (opens the official source in a new tab)

If your condition makes you physically unable to go to a dispensary, you may have up to two caregivers, who may buy medicinal cannabis without you present. A caregiver, typically a friend or family member you choose, must be a New Jersey resident and at least 18 years old, and must get a program ID card through the same registration process as patients. Caregivers who are not immediate family members must submit fingerprints for a criminal background check.

From official New Jersey sources

  • Registered patients whose condition makes them physically unable to go to a dispensary (ATC) may have up to two caregivers, who may buy medicinal cannabis without the patient present. Caregivers (opens the official source in a new tab)
  • Caregivers, typically a friend or family member chosen by the patient, can be added during or after registration and must get a program ID card through the same registration process as patients. Caregivers (opens the official source in a new tab)
  • A caregiver must be a New Jersey resident and at least 18 years old. Caregivers (opens the official source in a new tab)
  • Caregivers who are not immediate family members must submit fingerprints for a criminal background check. Caregivers (opens the official source in a new tab)

Buying as a registered patient

The program's patient FAQ says the most a patient or designated caregiver may buy is 85 grams (3 oz) for a 30-day period, that your authorizing practitioner sets your dosage, and that terminally ill patients are exempt from the maximum. The same FAQ also gives the maximum monthly allowance, taken as flower, as 84 grams, or 3 ounces, so confirm your exact limit with your practitioner. To buy from a dispensary's medical menu, a patient or caregiver must show their program ID.

From official New Jersey sources

  • The most a patient or designated caregiver may buy is 85 grams (3 oz) for a 30-day period, the practitioner sets the dosage, and terminally ill patients are exempt from the maximum. Patient FAQs (opens the official source in a new tab)
  • The same patient FAQ gives the maximum monthly allowance, taken as flower, as 84 grams, or 3 ounces. Patient FAQs (opens the official source in a new tab)
  • A patient or caregiver must show their program (MCP) ID to buy products on a dispensary's medical menu. Patient FAQs (opens the official source in a new tab)

Driving under the influence of cannabis is illegal in New Jersey whether the cannabis is used for recreational or medical purposes. Patients must not transport medicinal cannabis across state lines, and patients and caregivers are not permitted to grow cannabis or possess a cannabis plant.

From official New Jersey sources

  • Driving under the influence of cannabis is illegal in New Jersey, even though cannabis itself is legal for adults 21 and older. Don't Drive High (opens the official source in a new tab)
  • This applies whether cannabis is used for recreational or medical purposes, and an impaired driver can be charged with driving under the influence (DUI). Don't Drive High (opens the official source in a new tab)
  • Medical patients must not transport medicinal cannabis across state lines. Medicinal Cannabis Program (opens the official source in a new tab)
  • Medical patients and caregivers are not permitted to grow cannabis or possess a cannabis plant. Medicinal Cannabis Program (opens the official source in a new tab)

Where to get help

The program's Patient Services Unit can be reached at (609) 292-0424 or Medcannabis@crc.nj.gov. Rules change: check the official Medicinal Cannabis Program pages for current details.

From official New Jersey sources

  • The program's Patient Services Unit can be reached at (609) 292-0424 or Medcannabis@crc.nj.gov. Existing Patients (opens the official source in a new tab)

Common questions

Does health insurance cover medical cannabis in New Jersey?
No. Medicinal cannabis is not covered by any health plan in New Jersey.
How long is a patient ID card valid?
Both digital and physical ID cards are valid for two years. Digital cards are free, and a physical card costs $10.
